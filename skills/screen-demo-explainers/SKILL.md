# Screen demo explainers

Show a real task, its important interaction, and observed result. A viewer should know what to do and why it matters.

## Prepare the demonstration

Read the relevant change and run the workflow in a local or demo environment. Identify the initial state, shortest useful action sequence, and observable success condition. Use synthetic or authorized demo data. Keep credentials, notifications, personal tabs, and unrelated private information out of the recording.

Write a shot list: what is on screen, the action, spoken/captioned explanation, and evidence demonstrated. Distinguish actual behavior from planned features. A clearly labeled mockup can explain a proposal, but cannot prove working software.

## Capture and edit

Use a supported screen-recording or browser-capture tool available in the environment. Follow its interaction constraints. If recording is unavailable, report the limitation and offer an explicitly labeled screenshot walkthrough; never claim it is a live recording.

Prefer a stable crop and readable UI over constant zooming. Pause for the viewer to locate a control, then act. Remove idle waits without hiding meaningful latency or failure. Retain enough context that the viewer knows where they are.

Add restrained callouts for the specific control, changed region, or output. Use real captures for the application and graphics for explanation. Avoid covering controls with captions. For vertical delivery, reframe around the task rather than shrinking an entire desktop into a phone frame.

## Voice and delivery

Use supplied narration or an available authorized local/TTS voice. Do not imitate a named speaker. Align cues to the actual recording. Use sentence-level captions without word alignment; do not fabricate timestamps. Silent captioned demonstrations are acceptable when requested or disclosed.

Watch the full export, listen if narrated, and compare its claimed outcome with the actual run. Check text at viewing size, callouts, cuts, audio, and private information. Keep a transcript and source evidence beside the video. Report recording or verification limits and deliver the file; publishing or sending it elsewhere requires authorization.
